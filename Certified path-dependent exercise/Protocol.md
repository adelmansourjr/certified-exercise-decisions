# Certified exercise under a shared pricing budget

## Question

Given a fixed number of expensive continuation-kernel evaluations across a batch, can we allocate them to decisions whose sign is unresolved? Compare adaptive exercise certificates with strong direct quadrature and independent learned price/continuation baselines. This follow-up replaces the earlier vanilla recombining tree with a path-dependent Bermudan arithmetic-average put that has two future exercise dates.

## Model and reference

Strike is one. State consists of current spot, the number and arithmetic mean of accumulated fixings, a risk-free rate, and time and volatility for each of the two future intervals. Future spots follow risk-neutral geometric Brownian motion with deterministic interval volatilities. At the intermediate date, the choice is immediate payoff based on the accumulated average or the discounted expected final payoff after one more fixing. Conditional on intermediate spot, that expectation is a scaled European put, so the root continuation requires only a one-dimensional integral. This reduction applies to this specific maturity structure.

The training and calibration reference uses composite twelve-node Gauss–Legendre integration on 32 and 64 normal-coordinate intervals over `[-9, 9]`. Replace near-zero decisions (absolute margin at most 1e-5) and disagreements greater than 4e-6 between resolutions with independent SciPy adaptive quadrature. For test states, integrate every case using SciPy adaptive quadrature. Compare 256-piece Gauss–Legendre quadrature to the adaptive estimate on the first eight cases and up to eight closest to the exercise boundary per block; enforce a 4e-6 agreement threshold. Audit the analytic terminal kernel using independent integration over the final lognormal fixing. A QUADPACK diagnostic triggers an extra comparison against 384-piece composite quadrature, with an absolute 2e-7 agreement threshold. The omitted normal-tail contribution is at most 2Φ(-9), since the discounted payoff is bounded by one.

The first execution of the protocol halted in block 3 because a composite reference estimate was 2.21e-7 below a valid fixed Jensen lower limit. Independent integration resolved the discrepancy. The reference-only correction above was made before calculating aggregate outcomes. Solver budgets, policies, seeds, training labels and metrics were unchanged.

## Fixed sample plan

Use twelve independent blocks generated from SeedSequence(7451962). Each block has 1,100 training states, 400 calibration states, and two 1,024-case test panels. In the interpolated panel, the spot is 0.65–1.31, the prior average is 0.72–1.08, the rate 0.018–0.13, both volatilities 0.12–0.43, and times 0.10–0.55 and 0.09–0.48 years. Fixing counts are 3, 7, or 15. The shifted panel uses fixing counts 7 or 15, a longer last date of 0.49–0.84, and changed volatilities of 0.26–0.61 and 0.44–0.69. The other marginals stay fixed. Each panel combines 512 unselected random states and 512 from a disjoint pool of 4,096 states whose seven-node pilot continuation margins are closest to zero among positive intrinsic payoffs. This is a designed boundary-focused challenge, not an estimate of market boundary prevalence. Report the random and enriched slices separately as well as pooled within each panel.

## Budget and baselines

Charge one unit per evaluation of the common intermediate payoff kernel, including endpoints, with a batch budget of 19 times the number of test states. Report achieved units and elapsed time separately as the kernel budget omits integration administration. The candidate begins with four truncated lognormal probability bins (nine evaluations), refines the interval with the largest bound contribution for each unresolved state, and stops when the bracket decides exercise or the batch allowance is exhausted. Split at the probability midpoint. If the batch is near exhaustion, pick unresolved states by largest possible regret from the current bracket. At most 36 leaves can be used per state. Unresolved states use the midpoint rule. Comparator methods are fixed 19-node Gauss–Hermite quadrature, fixed nine-bin convex brackets, and a strong two-stage method with an initial seven-node Gauss–Hermite rule and 32-node upgrades on the smallest absolute margins that fit within the batch budget. Zero-intrinsic exercise is never necessary for any method. A certificate uses a 1e-11 numerical guard around the mathematical lower and upper bounds. Primary comparison is regret for adaptive bounds vs fixed Gauss–Hermite and two-stage Gauss–Hermite across both panels. If superiority intervals cross zero, report certification coverage as the contribution and disclose the remaining regret without treating unresolved states as certified.

Train cubic ridge predictors on the identical 1,100 reference valuation calls per block. They predict either value above exercise payoff or signed continuation advantage using the same inputs. Calibrate on the same 400 cases, using the same 81 prespecified thresholds between −0.01 and +0.01 and exact regret. These comparisons isolate targets at equal label budget. The offline reference cost is separate from the online numerical-solver budget. Report mean one-step decision regret, wrong-action frequency at positive intrinsic value, value RMSE, certificate fraction and kernel evaluations. Decision regret means forced exercise/continuation now and optimal decisions thereafter. Perform 5,000 paired bootstrap draws over the twelve independent blocks for candidate-minus-comparator results. Unadjusted 95% intervals describe the fixed comparisons. Record numerical and outcome failures.

## Guarantee

Let the already accrued sum be H, and let the first future spot be x. Intermediate exercise is `(1-(H+x)/(n+1))_+`. The maturity payoff, with future return R>0 independent of x, is `(1-(H+(1+R)x)/(n+2))_+`. Both are convex nonincreasing functions of x. Discounted expectation preserves both properties, as does the pointwise maximum of the exercise and continuation values. The intermediate exercise-or-continue value is consequently convex. Its support ends no later than x=n+2−H. On each bounded interval of the lognormal distribution, conditional Jensen gives a lower continuation bound and the affine chord gives an upper bound evaluated at the same conditional mean. A sign-separated bracket proves the optimal root action for that model. This follows from convexity and is not a general theorem for American options, different payoff structures, or jointly stochastic future volatility.
